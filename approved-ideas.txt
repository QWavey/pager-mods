  [W1] WPA handshake capture + crack  [loud,keys]
        Auto-pick the busiest target, force reassociation, capture the 4-way handshake, export to hashcat/hcxtools, crack on-box or offload to a GPU rig.

        Pull WPA key material straight from APs with PMKID enabled — no client present, no deauth needed. Quiet.
  [W3] Karma / known-beacon twin  [loud,creds]
        Listen for probe requests, then become whatever SSID the client is calling for so it auto-associates to you.
  [W5] WPS Pixie-Dust  [loud,keys]

  [W6] Targeted deauth toolkit  [loud,disruptive]

## USB-C host-side / device-identity ops (Pager plugged into a target PC via USB-C)
  [U1] USB-C device clone (whitelist bypass)  [access]
        Present to the PC as a normal wired device (FritzBox-style), enumerate
        everything the PC hands the device over USB-C (MAC, DHCP identity,
        hostname, vendor), then prompt "got all the info - clone it to look
        like this device?" After cloning, the Pager carries the SAME identity
        onto the LAN. Use case: a whitelisted school PC (its NIC is allowed,
        yours isn't) - clone it, then plug the Pager into the school LAN with
        the whitelisted identity for internet access. Flow: unplug LAN from the
        PC -> plug USB-C into the PC (PC leaks its identity to the Pager) ->
        unplug -> plug the Pager into the school LAN as that device.

  [U2] DNS walled-garden redirect  [MITM]
        With the PC tethered over USB-C and the Pager on the LAN (bridge-like),
        run a DNS resolver that answers every lookup with ONE allowed page
        (local or a real internet page) and blocks the rest - a captive
        walled garden for the tethered host.

  [U3] Stealth intercept ("hanging" internet)  [MITM,stealth]
        Make the PC look like it has no/broken internet (requests appear to
        hang) while the Pager silently forwards the traffic to the internet,
        receives the responses, and withholds them from Windows - full silent
        interception with a plausible "network's down" cover story.

  [U4] Network topology mapper  [intel]
        Map the path from our LAN drop upwards: LAN -> switch -> router, or
        LAN -> wifi-router / repeater. Distinguish switch vs. repeater vs.
        gateway (TTL/hop analysis, LLDP/CDP, ARP, traceroute) and draw the tree.
